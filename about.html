About me

My name is Fadime Renée Stemmer (Pronunciation: /faˈdime ʁeːˈne: ˈʃtɛmɐ/), and I am currently a PhD candidate in the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI) Joint Program in Chemical Oceanography. I grew up in Southern Germany, obtained my Bachelors degree in Biochemistry from the Technical University of Munich and received a Masters degree in Chemical Oceanography from MIT and WHOI. I am a Biochemist who works on the intersection between proteomics and protein biochemistry, enzymology, and environmental microbial ecology. Working in the lab of Dr. Mak Saito I work to uncover the proteolytic breakdown of particulate organic matter in the ocean and its contribution to carbon, nitrogen, and trace metal cycling.

Passion for Protein Biochemistry and Climate Research

A biochemist and structural biologist by training I am fascinated by the world of proteins. These tiny, sub-cellular machines are what keep us and our planet alive - and yet we don't understand much about their functions and contributions to the earth and climate system. Especially the role of enzymes produced by marine microorganisms to climate relevant elemental cycles (C, N, trace metals) remains largely understudied.
